What does it all mean?
  • In sync: the feeder's clock is lined up with other feeders, so its timing can be used for positions. This is the normal state.
  • Waiting for sync: connected, but no other feeder has heard the same ADS-B aircraft as it yet. Normal for a minute or two after joining, and for a feeder far from all others. Nothing to do.
  • Clock problem: its timing keeps disagreeing with its neighbours. Usually a wrong location or antenna height in its settings, or an overloaded/unsteady computer. Ask the volunteer to check their latitude, longitude and altitude.
  • Location doesn't match: the aircraft it hears are far from where it says it is. Almost always a typo in its settings (e.g. a missing minus sign on longitude). Ask them to check.
  • Outside the area: it hears no aircraft inside the allowed area; the feeder check bans it automatically (and lifts that a week after it stops).
  • Map feed only: sends ADS-B for the map, but no MLAT client is connected. Fine, but it doesn't help with Mode A/C positions.
  • Coverage is rough: each working feeder covers a circle as far out as it has heard aircraft (feeders with problems don't count). Mode A/C positions need 4 feeders hearing the same aircraft, so the green area is where unknowns can be located. Low-flying aircraft are heard less far, so expect less at low altitude.
  • Mode S msg/s is what the feeder's MLAT client sends; ADS-B msg/s and positions/s are its map feed. A feeder near busy airspace with a good antenna shows more.
